Catching Up in Chicago

Elena and Mateo are catching up over coffee in Chicago. They both moved to the U.S. three years ago.

Elena: It's hard to believe we have been here for three years already. When I first arrived, I felt like my life was a total transplant. Everything felt temporary and transient.

Mateo: I know what you mean. I was so lost that first year. I spent so much time trying to dismantle my old habits just to fit in. I even tried to change my accent because I thought it deviated too much from the "standard" here.

Elena: Did it work?

Mateo: Not really. I've realized lately that my accent is inherent to who I am. Since I stopped fighting it, I have felt much more confident. I have finally incorporated my Colombian roots into my professional life instead of hiding them.

Elena: That's a huge shift. I have experienced something similar. Last year, I recalibrated my goals. I thought my old teaching skills were redundant here, but I have recently discovered that being bilingual is my greatest strength. It has revitalized my passion for education.

Mateo: It sounds like you have transfigured your struggle into a new career path. We were so worried about making mistakes when we first landed, but we have learned to improvise through the chaos!

3 · Write Your Move to America

Write 6–9 sentences about your move to America. Describe some of the changes you have made. Use past simple AND present perfect, plus at least 3 lesson vocabulary words.

📅 Past simple — finished actions at a specific time: "I arrived in 2022." / "I dismantled my old routine."
🌉 Present perfect — past with a bridge to now: "I have lived here for three years." / "I have recalibrated my goals."

  • 6–9 sentences
  • Use BOTH past simple AND present perfect
  • At least 3 lesson vocabulary words (transient, dismantle, recalibrate, redundant, incorporate, revitalize, deviate, inherent, transfigure, improvise…)
